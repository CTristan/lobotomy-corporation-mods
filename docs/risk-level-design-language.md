# Lobotomy Corporation — Risk-Level Design Language (Behavior Analysis)

> **What this is.** A reverse-engineering of the game's *implicit* design language for the five
> Risk Levels (ZAYIN → TETH → HE → WAW → ALEPH), read from the **behavior code** of every
> base-game abnormality. It characterizes the *hard half* of Risk Level — the per-creature C#
> behavior that cannot be reduced to a metric — and synthesizes per-tier qualitative guidelines.
> The numbers (HP, work probabilities, PE yield, defenses) appear only as **context**.
>
> **Two uses:** (1) a reference for authoring tier-appropriate new abnormalities; (2) the
> qualitative half of a risk-level audit. This is **not** a difficulty score or a formula.
>
> **Ground rules followed throughout:** every pattern cites the specific creatures and code
> locations behind it; patterns are stated as prevalence ("N of M"), never absolutes; exceptions
> are surfaced per tier; and where a tier does not cohere, that is reported plainly rather than
> dressed up as a rule.

**Data sources.** Roster + stats: `external/snapshots/LobotomyModManager/1.3.9/…/BaseMod/`
(`BaseList.txt`, `BaseCreatures/{Name}_stat.txt` — the bundled BaseMod is the base-game data).
Player-facing risk + code (`codeNo`): `external/snapshots/LobotomyCorp_vanilla/…/Language/en/creatures/{Name}_en.xml`.
Behavior classes: `external/decompiled/Assembly-CSharp/{Class}.cs` (115 classes extend `CreatureBase`).

**How risk is encoded.** `RiskLevel` enum (`Assembly-CSharp/RiskLevel.cs`): `ZAYIN=0 … ALEPH=4`.
Each `_stat.txt` carries a numeric `<riskLevel>` (= enum + 1); each `_en.xml` carries the
player-facing string. **The player-facing string is treated as authoritative**; the few
creatures where the stat numeric disagrees are flagged below. Behavior is bound at runtime by
reflection from the `<script>` field in the stat file to the matching C# class.

## Per-tier profiles

### ZAYIN — Tier Design Language

ZAYIN is the game's lowest risk tier, and the 11 in-scope abnormalities show a strikingly coherent design philosophy: **danger comes from the player's own interaction, not from the creature.** These are containment units you are meant to work safely; the threat is what happens when you work them *wrong*.

**Behavioral hallmarks (prevalence over 11):**

- **Non-escaping by design — 10 of 11** (OneBadManyGood, DontTouchMe, LightsHammer, Wellcheers, Bald, Fairy, ResetMirror, IronMaiden, YouMustHappy, PromiseAndFaith). All ten declare `escapeable=false` and `qliphoth=0` and provide no `Escape`/`UniqueEscape` override (e.g. `OneBadManyGood_stat.txt:69-70`, `DontTouchMe_stat.txt:5-6`, `IronMaiden_stat.txt:22-23`). A ZAYIN that breaches is the exception, not the rule.
- **Danger flows through the work/interaction window — 11 of 11.** Every unit's harm is gated on the player opening a window, finishing work, or invoking a special skill — never on roaming or breach combat. DontTouchMe's `OnOpenWorkWindow` *is* the attack (`DontTouchMe.cs:186-206`); Wellcheers reacts to the work outcome (`Wellcheers.cs:167-192`); IronMaiden only ticks damage during work (`IronMaiden.cs:84-106`); even the breach-capable PinkCorps is driven toward breach by over-using its own protection skill (`PinkCorps.cs:311-336`).
- **Reward-shaped traps — 8 of 11** (OneBadManyGood, Wellcheers, Bald, Fairy, YouMustHappy, ResetMirror, PromiseAndFaith, PinkCorps). The defining ZAYIN shape is a tangible benefit fused to a punishing downside: Fairy's 10%/s heal buff that kills the agent if they go idle (`FairyBuf.cs:43,54`); Wellcheers' heal-drink that becomes a death-drink on a bad result (`Wellcheers.cs:188-191,288`); YouMustHappy's quadratic stat-buff gamble that marks over-buffed agents to die (`YouMustHappy.cs:101-108,182-191`); PromiseAndFaith's weapon upgrade-or-destroy roll (`PromiseAndFaith.cs:17`).
- **Single-target harm — 6 of 11** (OneBadManyGood, Wellcheers, Fairy, IronMaiden, YouMustHappy, PromiseAndFaith). When a ZAYIN hurts you, it usually hurts exactly the one worker who interacted (`Wellcheers.cs:288`, `IronMaiden.cs:93`, `YouMustHappy.cs:160-164`).
- **Loss-of-control as a hazard — 4 of 11** (LightsHammer, Wellcheers, YouMustHappy, PinkCorps). `LoseControl`/forced-move/uncontrollable conversion recurs as a soft punishment (`LightsHammer.cs:248-291`, `Wellcheers.cs:194-203`, `YouMustHappy.cs:162`, `PinkCorps.cs:401-410`). *(Verification correction: Fairy was dropped from this count — `FairyBuf.cs:46-54` kills a buffed agent who goes idle, which is a death-on-idle constraint, not loss of player control.)*
- **Stateful via interaction counters, never a breach counter — 8 of 11** (DontTouchMe, LightsHammer, Wellcheers, Bald, ResetMirror, IronMaiden, YouMustHappy, PromiseAndFaith). These units carry per-agent/per-session accumulators that escalate consequences *without* ever leading to a breach: Bald's 3-level stack (`Bald.cs:98`), IronMaiden's `_tickCount` escalator (`IronMaiden.cs:6,94`), DontTouchMe's 5-clicks-in-10s force-exit queue (`DontTouchMe.cs:320-333`), YouMustHappy's 6-use death counter (`YouMustHappy.cs:148,160`).

**Stat context.** Stat files are low-signal and nearly uniform — 10 of 11 are `escapeable=false`/`qliphoth=0`, defenses (where present) are flat 1.0 to all four R/W/B/P types, and several special/kit units ship no HP/defense/work-prob block at all. The `specialDamage` entries (W or R 10-14) are largely display values; the actual lethal outcomes are unconditional `agent.Die()` calls, and the escalation math that matters lives in code constants, not stats. **For a ZAYIN, the behavior class is the spec — the numbers tell you almost nothing.**

**Where the danger comes from: behavior, overwhelmingly.** There is no stat-driven threat here. Every meaningful hazard is a scripted interaction rule — a work-outcome branch, a use-count threshold, an idle-detection kill, a timing gamble — and knowing the numbers without knowing the rule keeps no one alive.

**Exceptions — where the design language actually lives:**

- **PinkCorps** is the *only* breach-capable unit in the tier. It carries a real qliphoth counter (`PinkCorps_stat.txt:85`, qliphoth=3, alongside a contradictory dual `escapeable` flag at lines 43/84); at zero it transforms into 4 ALEPH-class BlackCorps (`BlackCorps.cs:46`) that explode Sefira-wide for W 30-40 and cascade-breach other abnormalities (`BlackCorps.cs:23,230`; `PinkCorps.cs:498,684-718`). It single-handedly breaks the non-escaping and no-cascade norms — a ZAYIN that ends as a facility-wide ALEPH event.
- **DontTouchMe** breaks the single-target and reward norms: it offers no benefit, only a 50/50 facility-wide kill-or-panic on opening its work window (`DontTouchMe.cs:197,236`), with click-escalation paths that zero every creature's qliphoth counter (`DontTouchMe.cs:251`) or force-quit the run (`DontTouchMe.cs:344`).
- **LightsHammer** inverts the tier entirely — a counter-breach *tool* that spawns invincible warrior-clones to kill *other* escaped abnormalities (`LightsHammer.cs:118-183,248-291`); its only cost is the seized worker.
- **PromiseAndFaith** is the only unit with `employee_effects=['none']` — the stake is a destroyed-or-upgraded weapon (`PromiseAndFaith.cs:17`), not flesh.
- **ResetMirror** and **Bald** are the most benign: ResetMirror only randomly redistributes a worker's stats (`ResetMirror.cs:39-123`), and Bald's facility-wide stack-3 effect merely changes hair and fully restores mental (`Bald.cs:127,144`) — a facility-wide reach that is purely beneficial.

### TETH — Tier Design Profile (22 abnormalities)

TETH is the tier where the **gimmick is the threat**. Bodies are weak and counters are tiny; what makes a TETH dangerous is a *rule* — work it the wrong way, with the wrong worker, or at the wrong moment, and it punishes you, usually fatally. The work panel is simultaneously the control surface and the trap.

**Roster:** Dummy, Matchgirl, 1.76mhz, SpiderMom, BeautyBeast, StraitJacket, SmallBird, OldLady, LadyLookingAtWall, BloodBath, Cosmos, ArmorCreature, ShyThing, Bunny, Sakura, Baku, Ppodae, DesireHeart, Theresia, ProphecyOfSkin, HealthBracelet, JusticeReceiver.

#### Behavioral hallmarks (by prevalence)

- **Work-conditional threat — 20 of 22.** The hazard is gated on *how* you work it (feeling-state, timing, work-type, or agent stat), never a fixed timer. Examples: StraitJacket breaches on a single BAD-feeling work end (`StraitJacket.cs:107-120`); Bunny breaches if work ends BAD *or* finishes in ≤40s (`Bunny.cs:68-75`); SpiderMom kills on Prudence-type work or low Prudence (`SpiderMom.cs:94-96`); ShyThing swings work damage 0.5×–2× by a frozen face (`ShyThing.cs:90-120`). Only OldLady (facility-timing, not per-work) and ProphecyOfSkin (delayed death-tag) fall outside this.
- **Cannot breach containment — 14 of 22 (escapeable=false).** The default TETH never leaves its cell; it kills or controls *in-room* or pulls the worker *to it*. SpiderMom drags the worker into its nest (`SpiderMom.cs:155-160`), Sakura attracts workers to itself (`Sakura.cs:159-172`), BloodBath/BeautyBeast/ArmorCreature kill at the workstation. Breaching is the **minority** behavior here (8 of 22 escape).
- **Qliphoth-counter gimmick — 12 of 22.** A small counter (1–4), drained by mismanaged work, fires the shared engine hook `script.ActivateQliphothCounter()` at zero (`CreatureModel.cs:1546`), which the class overrides into either an escape (StraitJacket `:122-126`, Bunny `:121-125`, Matchgirl `:114-118`, Ppodae `:151-165`) or an *in-cell* skill (1.76mhz noise `Mhz_1_76.cs:221-231`, LadyLookingAtWall discharge `:128-148`, Sakura attract `:104-108`). Counters are deliberately tiny — StraitJacket and Bunny breach on a single bad work.
- **Lethal worker punishment — 11 of 22.** Misworking does not chip HP; it kills outright via scripted `Die()` / hp-zero / sacrifice — almost always single-target. SpiderMom (`:181`), BeautyBeast (`:141-161`), BloodBath (`:84-95`), ArmorCreature (`:147-156`), Baku (`:171-184`), Bunny one-shot 500 R (`:220-241`), Sakura sacrifice (`:375-388`), Ppodae eats the worker (`:327-359`).
- **Hidden agent-stat / non-obvious gate — 7 of 22.** Safety hinges on a worker's Fortitude/Temperance/Prudence level or on a *trap* work-type the panel makes look attractive: SpiderMom's Prudence work shows 0.5–0.55 prob but is the kill trigger (`SpiderMom.cs:94-96` + `_stat.txt:21-27`); BeautyBeast's best-probability Pale work is what kills on the 2nd cast (`BeautyBeast.cs:8,87-104`); BloodBath/Baku/ArmorCreature key on lowest-tier Fortitude/Temperance.
- **Reward layered on the same dial — 8 of 22.** The mechanic that punishes also rewards, making these risk/reward sliders: BloodBath success-cube bonus (`:47-56`), ArmorCreature 4-tier EGO gift (`:191-216`), Sakura full-facility heal on recovery (`:326-338`), Theresia +10 mental/5s dept heal (`:43-48`), ShyThing's healing face (`:122-136`).
- **Self-recontaining breach — 5 of 22.** When TETH *does* breach, it tends to resolve itself: Matchgirl self-suppresses after one explosion (`:299`), OldLady auto-resets after the grab (`:74-79`), Ppodae eats one worker then walks home and self-suppresses (`:387-422`), Sakura resets after the sacrifice cycle (`:245-251`). The damage event is the cost, not an open-ended rampage.

#### Stat context

TETH is stat-light by design. HP clusters 100–330 (Matchgirl 120, Cosmos 230, Bunny 330 at the top), and several members have *no* HP/defense block because they are tools or stationary skill-objects. Breach damage is bimodal: trivial chip (SmallBird 1, 1.76mhz 4/3s, Baku 3–5 W, Ppodae 3–4 R, LadyLookingAtWall 18–24 W) versus rare one-shot bursts (Matchgirl fixed 300 R, Bunny fixed 500 R, SmallBird Mad-mode 800–1200 R). Qliphoth counters are uniformly small (1–4). Defenses are mostly flat 1.0 across R/W/B/P. The numbers are scaffolding for the gimmick.

#### Where the danger comes from

**Behavior, overwhelmingly.** A TETH at rest is near-harmless; the threat is a *rule* you can violate — wrong work type, wrong-mood work, an under-leveled worker, a mistimed release. The damage numbers exist only to make the punishment land (often a flat one-shot or a scripted death), not to create attrition pressure. Read every TETH as "what is the rule, and what happens when I break it" rather than "how hard does it hit."

#### Exceptions — where the design language actually lives

- **Tool sub-group (5 of 22): DesireHeart, Theresia, ProphecyOfSkin, HealthBracelet, JusticeReceiver.** These are `workType=kit` equip/channel/oneshot items, not cell creatures. The breach/escape lens does not apply; all "danger" is self-inflicted on the single equipping/reading/channeling agent (e.g. JusticeReceiver `:64-82`, HealthBracelet `:54-95`, DesireHeart `:75-84`). Theresia is even net-positive (a dept heal station). Flag these as a sub-group, not averaged into the tier's tendencies. ProphecyOfSkin is also the tier's only `punishes_wrong_action=false` member — its danger is a *delayed* death-tag, not a work-time mistake.
- **Dummy** — the tier's one harmless creature: a tutorial prop whose "escape" is a scripted walk + notice with zero damage (`DummyCreature.cs:43-54`). It carries the qliphoth/escape skeleton with none of the consequence.
- **1.76mhz** — breaks single-target/in-room: its qliphoth-zero event is a facility-wide spreading noise (`Mhz_1_76.cs:277-309`), area-denial recontained by working the counter back up, not by suppression.
- **SmallBird** — the tier's biggest outlier: passively breaches off facility-wide events (any panic, 20% on any work-start, `:677-699`), is not auto-suppressable, has a lethal 800–1200 Mad mode if mishandled (`:140-148,454-479`), and feeds the Apocalypse Bird ordeal (`:702-744`). High cascade risk well beyond TETH norms.
- **Sakura** — only facility-wide attract-and-sacrifice member while still escapeable=false (`:159-172`), with a unique full-facility heal reward and a manual 5-click rescue (`Uncontrollable_Sakura.cs:84-110`).
- **ShyThing** — pure no-stakes work puzzle: can't breach, can't kill, only chips Black work-damage by a random face (`:59-136`). Lowest-consequence non-tool creature.
- **OldLady** — only counter driven by the *rest of the facility* (an observer on every other creature's work, `:81-92`), refilled by working her while calm — a management-timing hazard, not a per-work mistake.

### HE — Tier Profile (20 abnormalities)

HE is a **behavior-defined tier, not a numbers tier**. The 20 creatures share remarkably little in their raw stats — HP runs 200-830 where it exists at all, per-hit damage is modest (mostly single-digit to ~30), and several flagship members carry no HP/defense block whatsoever. What unifies HE is a small set of *mechanical shapes*: a tiny work-fed danger counter, scripted instant-death, and the recurring "your instinct is the trap" inversion. The danger comes from **behavior**, not numerals.

#### Behavioral hallmarks (with prevalence)

- **Work-fed Qliphoth counter as the danger gate — 12 of 20** (RedShoes, SingingMachine, Helper, Rudolph, NamelessFetus, GalaxyBoy, Butterfly, LookAtMe, Scarecrow, Lumberjack, Porccu, Freischutz). The counter is decremented by *work outcome* — a bad/neutral mood result, or a wrong-stat agent, on a work session pushes it toward zero. Counters are deliberately tiny: five sit at max 1 (Scarecrow, Lumberjack, RedShoes, SingingMachine, NamelessFetus) and five at max 2 (Helper, Rudolph, Butterfly, Porccu, LookAtMe), so **one qualifying work session can trip the danger state**. Cited: `Helper.cs:583-607` (BAD 70%/NORM 50%/GOOD 0% SubQliphothCounter), `Butterfly.cs:258-273` (-1 Justice<3, -1 Fortitude>3, -1@80% BAD), `Scarecrow.cs:121-142`, `NamelessFetus.cs:141-158`, `Porccu.cs:162-173`.

- **Qliphoth-zero fires `ActivateQliphothCounter` -> Escape() or a scripted lethal state — 12 of 20** (same set). The engine dispatches at `CreatureModel.cs:1543-1546` when the counter hits 0; the class overrides it. Seven escape/relocate (`Helper.cs:168`, `Butterfly.cs:252-256`, `Scarecrow.cs:63-67`, `Rudolph.cs:203-207`, `LookAtMe.cs:77-81`, `Porccu.cs:92-96`, plus RedShoes via attract), while four turn the zero-event into a *stationary* lethal state — NamelessFetus cries (`NamelessFetus.cs:131-139`), GalaxyBoy cries (`GalaxyBoy.cs:191-196`), Freischutz snipes (`Freischutz.cs:75-79`), SingingMachine arms a room-entry suicide trap (`SingingMachine.cs:80`), and Lumberjack ambushes the next entrant (`Lumberjack.cs:130-143`).

- **Scripted instant-death, bypassing HP attrition — 11 of 20** (HappyTeddy, RedShoes, NamelessFetus, GalaxyBoy, Butterfly, Lumberjack, Porccu, ScytheClock, SnowQueen, ResearcherNote, OtherWorldPortrait). These do not whittle an employee's HP; they call `.Die()` outright. Cited: `ScytheClock.cs:325-356` (random facility-wide `workerModel.Die()`), `SnowQueen.cs:412-428` (`KillTwoAgents`), `HappyTeddy.cs:85-111` (DeadType.SPECIAL + `lastAgent.Die()`), `Porccu.cs:175-190` (GOOD-result low-Temperance worker executed), `Butterfly.cs:562-574` (panicking agents killed on hit), `OtherWorldPortrait.cs:313-339`, `ResearcherNote.cs:80`.

- **Cannot physically breach (escapeable=false) — 11 of 20** (HappyTeddy, SingingMachine, NamelessFetus, GalaxyBoy, ScytheClock, SnowQueen, Freischutz, ResearcherNote, BigTreeSap, Shelter, OtherWorldPortrait). The threat is delivered through in-room work interaction or a conscripted/sniped victim, not a roaming creature. Cited: `HappyTeddy_stat.txt:35,70`, `SnowQueen_stat.txt:43,72`, `ScytheClock_stat.txt:5` + `CreatureBase.cs:420-422` (Escape/UniqueEscape empty, never overridden — verified).

- **Inverted work puzzle ("good action is the trap") — 6 of 20** (Scarecrow, Butterfly, Porccu, LookAtMe, GalaxyBoy, RedShoes). The expected-safe action triggers the danger. Send LOW-Prudence agents to Scarecrow (`Scarecrow.cs:121-132`, verified — high Prudence breaches it); keep Fortitude LOW for Butterfly (`Butterfly.cs:258-273`, verified — high Fortitude breaches it); do NOT watch LookAtMe during work (`LookAtMe.cs:122-129,157-179`, verified — observing it is the only counter-drain); a GOOD work result with a low-Temperance worker gets that worker killed by Porccu (`Porccu.cs:175-190`).

- **Breaches into a roaming/mobile attacker — 8 of 20** (RedShoes, SingingMachine, Helper, Rudolph, LittleWitch, Butterfly, Lumberjack, Scarecrow). When HE does escape, it tends to traverse departments hunting or grazing workers. Cited: `Helper.cs:187-238`, `Rudolph.cs:6-111`, `Lumberjack.cs:179-242`, `Scarecrow.cs:196-216`, `LittleWitchMonster.cs:152-238`.

- **Mind-controls / conscripts an employee as the delivery mechanism — 6 of 20** (RedShoes, SingingMachine, Lumberjack, SnowQueen, ResearcherNote, NamelessFetus). Cited: `RedShoesSkill.cs:259-301` (possession into killer puppet), `SingingMachine.cs:207-211` (worker made immortal attacker), `ResearcherNote.cs:34-44` (forced suicide animation).

#### Stat context

Combat numbers are modest and inconsistent and serve mainly to answer "which weapon suppresses it" and "how few work sessions until it triggers." Per-hit damage spans 3-30 across mixed types; HP runs 200-830 where present (and is absent entirely for HappyTeddy, ScytheClock, SnowQueen, and the four kit/tool abnormalities). Qliphoth ceilings are intentionally low — eleven breach-capable creatures sit at 1-2, with only Freischutz (3) and GalaxyBoy (5) higher. Defenses are frequently flat-neutral (HappyTeddy, NamelessFetus, GalaxyBoy, SnowQueen, Freischutz all 1.0). The lethality is in the script, not the spreadsheet.

#### Where the danger comes from

**Behavior.** No numeric threshold defines HE; the unifying threat is the *mechanism*: a small work-fed counter that snaps to a scripted lethal state, instant-death calls that ignore HP, and inverted-intuition work puzzles where the obvious move is the trigger.

#### Sub-group: kit/tool (passive, no-breach) abnormalities — 4 of 20

ResearcherNote, BigTreeSap, Shelter, and OtherWorldPortrait are equippable/channel tools (workType=kit), not worked containers, and should not be averaged into the tier's tendencies. Three are still lethal — ResearcherNote (`ResearcherNote.cs:60-103`, delayed suicide explosion, 30 R splash) and BigTreeSap (`BigTreeSap.cs:33-49`, greed-escalated suicide bomb) detonate the wielder and splash the passage; OtherWorldPortrait (`OtherWorldPortrait.cs:63-101`) reroutes amplified damage onto a random other employee. **Shelter is the lone benign member of the entire tier** (`Shelter.cs:35-75`) — it heals its worker, makes them invincible, and lowers facility risk by zeroing a neighbor's qliphoth counter.

#### Exceptions — where the design language bends

- **Shelter** — the only purely-benevolent HE; zero damage, heals, reduces facility risk (`Shelter.cs:35-75`).
- **ScytheClock** — no counter, no work gimmick; its facility-wide random kill fires only on a player pause+spacebar meta-input while in BAD mood (`ScytheClock.cs:325-356`, verified), and persists kill count across days.
- **GalaxyBoy** — inverts the counter (working it RAISES it); danger is a slow single-target Die()-loop plus an Energy drain, not a breach (`GalaxyBoy.cs:246-257`).
- **LittleWitch** — delayed, decoupled breach: a dormant heart buff converts the agent into a *separately spawned* roaming monster only when they later work a different abnormality (`LittleWitchBuf.cs:72-97`).
- **HappyTeddy** — the simplest member: kills the agent who works it twice in a row, no counter/mood/stat gate (`HappyTeddy.cs:47-58`, verified).
- **Freischutz** — has the second-highest counter (3) yet never escapes; fires a random facility-wide sniper shot and auto-rearms (`Freischutz.cs:75-121`).

### WAW

WAW is where Lobotomy Corporation's danger stops being about numbers and becomes about *learning a machine*. Almost every box in this tier has a small, fragile Qliphoth counter and a specific mechanic that drains it; the player's job is to learn that mechanic before the counter hits zero. The tier reads as a catalogue of management puzzles, each with its own breach trigger, its own lethality delivery, and its own suppression weakness.

**Behavioral hallmarks (prevalence over 26 in-scope abnormalities):**

- **Qliphoth-counter breach is the spine — 20/26.** The shared engine pattern is an `ActivateQliphothCounter()` override fired when the counter reaches zero, which either escapes the body or detonates its danger. Verified verbatim in `MagicalGirl.cs:454-458` and `BigBird.cs:143-147` (both `=> Escape()`), `Alriune.cs:139-143`, and `SnowWhite.cs:191`. The hook is reused even by non-escaping boxes: `QueenBee.cs:190` (`=> EmitSpore()`), `YoungPrince.cs:99-103` (`=> Attract()`), and the inverted `BloodyTree.cs:101-113` (kills the working agent, then escapes). `MeatIdol.cs:106-111` weaponizes it the other way — overworking past 90s zeroes *every* creature's counter. *(Verification correction: recomputed from 23 to 20 — the source list double-counted `MagicalGirl_2`, and the two kit-tools `Yang` (breaches only when its twin Yin does, with no own `ActivateQliphothCounter`) and `MeatIdol` (its counter-zero hits *other* creatures, not itself) are excluded; both sit in the tool/kit sub-group below.)*
- **Work feeling-state is the usual lever — 14/26.** The recurring idiom is `OnWorkCoolTimeEnd` rolling to `SubQliphothCounter` based on the mood the work finished in: BAD ~70% / NORM ~50% / GOOD 0%. Confirmed in `SnowWhite.cs:508-520`, `QueenBee.cs:172-184`, `LongBird.cs:418-435`, `MagicalGirl_2.cs:517-533`, and `Wraith.cs:152-158`. `BloodyTree.cs:139-156` inverts it (GOOD work *adds* counter); `Alriune.cs:120-137` inverts it differently (NEUTRAL is the only safe outcome).
- **Breach is mobile, not room-bound — 14/26.** Once loose, these threats teleport or roam across all opened passages: `SnowWhite.cs:392`, `Alriune.cs:252-287`, `MagicalGirl_2.cs:355-398`, `Shark.cs:358-398`, `BloodyTree.cs:212-246`, `BigBadWolf.cs:940`, `FengYun.cs:340`, `Wraith.cs:121-131`. Suppression becomes a chase, and corridors fill with collateral damage.
- **Lethality bypasses HP/defense — 11/26.** Scripted death is preferred over a typed hit: `BigBird.cs:442-469` forces `target.Die()`; `AlriuneAuraBuf.cs:34` and `YoungPrince.cs:672-676` call `.Die()` on controlled/panicking victims; `BloodyTree.cs:101-113` kills the worker pre-breach; `Shark.cs:269` (150 R) and `FengYun.cs:461` (250 R + gut-explosion) effectively one-shot; `ReverseClock.cs:263-287` rolls 50% Die / 50% Panic facility-wide with no damage type.
- **Combat delegated to children or controlled staff — 7/26.** The body often never swings: `BlackSwan.cs:228-241` is untargetable and spawns the fighting `BlackSwanSister`; `QueenBeeWorker.cs:100-130` self-replicates per kill; `Yggdrasil.cs:774-823` turns corpses into Saplings; `ViscusSnakeBuf.cs:287-302` mind-controls the host into the attacker; `AuthorNote.cs:337-391` forces your own staff to kill each other; `Piano.cs:526-581` births a corridor PianoCreature.
- **Cascade coupling, often bidirectional — 8/26.** `BigBird.cs:307-311`/`1013-1049` (emergency + facility deaths force-zero its counter, can fuse into Apocalypse Bird), `RedHood.cs:1068-1084` (other escapes drain it), `BigBadWolf.cs:859-872` (drains *neighbors'* counters), `BlackSwan.cs:180-221`, `FengYun.cs:118`/`640`, `Yin.cs:198-206`. WAW is where one breach starts pulling others down with it.

**Tool/kit sub-group (4/26, do NOT average into combat tendencies):** `MeatIdol` (`MeatIdol_stat.txt:7-9`, channel), `HellTrain` (`HellTrain_stat.txt:8-9`, oneshot), `Yang` (`Yang_stat.txt:7`, equip), `ReverseClock` (`ReverseClock_stat.txt:7-9`, oneshot). These are `workType=kit` abnormalities — equipped or one-shot-worked, not contained against a breach. Yang is outright benevolent (heals + reflects White, `Yang.cs:157`/`311`; stat riskLevel is even 2/TETH). Treating these as "WAW combatants" misrepresents the tier.

**Stat context:** HP, breach damage, and defenses are all wide spreads tuned per-creature, not tier constants. Bruisers run 1000-2000 HP (BigBadWolf 2000, BigBird 1600); lethal threats are often deliberately fragile (Shark 200, LongBird ~280, ViscusSnake 300); tool/script units carry no real HP block. Damage ranges from instant-kill scripts down to small sustained ticks (Wraith 40 R, QueenBee 8 R/2s). Counters are small (mostly 1-2), so margins are thin, and each box has a single tuned defensive weak-spot (SnowWhite B=0, Alriune W-immune, Wraith R-immune, LongBird P=2.0). The numbers exist to force the player to *learn* each box.

**Where the danger comes from:** overwhelmingly **behavior**, not numbers. A WAW abnormality is dangerous because of how it breaches (mood, observation, agent choice, or a paired creature's state), how it kills (scripted death, mind-control, self-replicating children), and how far it spreads — not because of a big HP or damage stat. The numbers gate the mechanics; the mechanics are the threat.

**Exceptions (where the design language actually lives):** **OrangeTree** is inert — a reused tutorial `DummyCreature` script with no combat or threat (`DummyCreature.cs:30-54`), breaking every hallmark. **KnightOfDespair** and **AuthorNote** have no Qliphoth counter at all (`KnightOfDespair.cs:613` HasRoomCounter false; AuthorNote uses a hidden charge) — and KnightOfDespair makes *good* work the trap. **ViscusSnake** inverts the safety rule entirely: good results and wounded agents *raise* infection odds (`ViscusSnake.cs:100-112`). **Shark** keys its counter to agent properties, not feeling-state (`Shark.cs:177-188`). **BloodyTree** inverts and observation-gates its counter (`BloodyTree.cs:76-99`). **MeatIdol** is a no-breach sacrifice timer. **Yang** is a healer that only breaches when its twin does.

### ALEPH

The 6 in-scope ALEPH abnormalities (NullThing, SilentOrchestra, DangoCreature, Censored, BlueStar, SlimeGirl) are the tier with the most uniform *skeleton* and the most varied *flesh*. Every one of them shares a breach spine, a state machine, and a facility-wide threat radius — but each builds a wholly distinct trap on top of it. The design language here is: **a fragile management puzzle whose failure state is a scripted, escalating, facility-wide catastrophe.**

#### Behavioral hallmarks

- **Qliphoth-zero breach (6/6 — all).** Every ALEPH overrides `ActivateQliphothCounter()` to call `Escape()` directly, and ships a deliberately tiny counter so the breach gate is discipline, not attrition: NullThing's is **1** (a single bad work breaches it — `Nothing.cs:255-258`, `NullThing_stat.txt:89`); SilentOrchestra/Dango/Censored/BlueStar sit at **2** (`SilentOrchestra.cs:327-331`, `DangoCreature.cs:311-325`, `Censored.cs:168-174`, `BlueStar.cs:153`); SlimeGirl at **3** (`SlimeGirl.cs:405-407`).
- **Mismanagement drains the counter toward breach (6/6 — all).** The counter goes *down* on bad outcomes, not up on neutral ones — bad-mood work, broken mental, wrong-stat or hurt agents, and over-long sessions all decrement it: `Nothing.cs:343-347` (Justice<=3), `SilentOrchestra.cs:224-231` (non-GOOD mood), `DangoCreature.cs:206-213` (BAD mood, plus hurt-agent/death paths), `Censored.cs:137,144` (mental<=0 / BAD), `BlueStar.cs:120-132` (>=60s session / Prudence<=4), `SlimeGirl.cs:363-369` (BAD or NORM mood).
- **Multi-form / multi-phase state machine (6/6 — all).** None breach into a single mode; each escalates through forms, movements, or levels with per-phase stats and defenses: NullThing's 5-form enum `WORKER_ROOM/LV1/WORKER_ESCAPE/EGG/LV2` (`Nothing.cs:62-69`), SilentOrchestra's `NONE/MOVEMENT1-4/FINALE` concert (`SilentOrchestra.cs:7-15,369-409`), Dango's four-level corpse-eating ladder that also levels *down* (`DangoCreature.cs:7-13,586-611,837-848`), Censored's sacrifice/dead-scene phase flags (`Censored.cs:49-55,375`), BlueStar's dead-scene queue and teleport state (`BlueStar.cs:153-189,329-342`), and SlimeGirl's `Phase`+`State` enums atop a self-propagating infestation (`SlimeGirl.cs:26-36`).
- **Facility-wide reach on breach (6/6 — all).** Four hunt or roam (NullThing `Nothing.cs:591-636`, Dango `DangoCreature.cs:496-507`, Censored `CensoredCreatureBase.cs:153`, SlimeGirl `SlimeGirl.cs:537-590` + `SlimeCreature.cs:282`); two teleport (SilentOrchestra `SilentOrchestra.cs:547-552`, BlueStar `BlueStar.cs:222-237`). The cell never contains the breach.
- **High cascade risk (6/6 — all).** A single breach chains into facility-wide death via corridor AoE (`Nothing.cs:864-914`), 100% energy drain plus mass execution of panicking staff (`SilentOrchestra.cs:613-620,632-642`), a corpse-fed death spiral (`DangoCreature.cs:709-740,889-936`), child-creature spawns (`Censored.cs:441`), a panic-feeds-kills loop (`BlueStar.cs:239-264`), or a self-spreading slime epidemic (`SlimeGirl.cs:251` + `SlimeTransformBuf.cs:19`).
- **Custom instant-kill / dead-scene that bypasses the HP model (4/6 — NullThing, Censored, BlueStar, SilentOrchestra).** These don't whittle HP, they call `Die()`: NullThing impersonates a killed worker (`Nothing.cs:349-366`), Censored's sacrifice zeroes a victim's HP (`Censored.cs:260`), BlueStar's `BLUESTAR_DEAD` buf kills on entry/overtime (`BlueStar.cs:103,173-189` + `BlueStarBuf.cs:34`), and SilentOrchestra's finale buf forces a suicide animation (`SilentOrchestra.cs:632-642`). DangoCreature and SlimeGirl kill only through ordinary AoE damage.
- **Self-healing during breach (3/6 — NullThing, Censored, SlimeGirl).** NullThing's LV2 Heal + damage-gated passive (`Nothing.cs:816-852,260-267`), Censored's +5% maxHP (`Censored.cs:323`), SlimeGirl's pawn-sacrifice +200 and full-heal-on-enrage (`SlimeGirl.cs:619-629`). SilentOrchestra, DangoCreature, and BlueStar do not regenerate.
- **Hard stat-gated entry/work (4/6 — NullThing, Censored, BlueStar, SilentOrchestra).** Below the threshold the agent is punished, not merely unsuccessful: forced panic at Fortitude<4 / level<=4 (`Nothing.cs:310-330`, `Censored.cs:126`), instant death at Temperance<=3 (`BlueStar.cs:103`), or zero work success below level 3 (`SilentOrchestra_stat.txt:7-34`).

#### Stat context

The numbers exist to make *mismanagement* lethal, not to make a sponge. HP clusters high (NullThing 2000, SilentOrchestra 2000, BlueStar 2200; Censored/SlimeGirl 1500) but DangoCreature starts at only 1000. Each pairs a single- or dual-element attack with a defense profile that dictates the suppression weapon: NullThing's LV2 is physical-immune (R0, needs W/B/P), SilentOrchestra and BlueStar deal White, Dango shifts R→B as it grows, Censored deals B+W, and SlimeGirl is pure Black. Work slots are scarce and slow (maxWorkCount 1-4, workSpeed 0.32-0.4), tightening the same fragile-counter puzzle that the behavior layer sets up.

#### Where the danger comes from

**Both, tightly interlocked.** The *numbers* (counters of 1-3, hard stat gates, element-locked defenses) define how easily you fail; the *behavior* (escalating state machines, facility-wide hunting, instant-kill scenes, self-propagating cascades) defines how catastrophic that failure is. Neither half is dangerous alone — a 2200-HP creature that stayed in its cell would be trivial, and a fragile counter without the scripted breach would be a non-event. ALEPH danger is the product of the two.

#### Exceptions — where the design language actually lives

- **SilentOrchestra** is the lone *self-resolving* breach (5/6 are hard recontain): it runs its concert and force-returns itself (`SilentOrchestra.cs:644-659`). It also doesn't hunt — it teleports once and emits stationary expanding zones — and uniquely drains all facility energy and forces normal game speed.
- **DangoCreature** breaks the high-HP shape (1000 base) and is the only ALEPH you can *weaken* mid-breach by knocking it below a level threshold (`DangoCreature.cs:837-848`); it also has the most environmentally fragile counter, dropping on every 10 cumulative facility deaths (`DangoCreature.cs:889-936`).
- **SlimeGirl** is the only one that can breach with *zero work done* — 50% pawn-infested workforce force-zeroes the counter (`SlimeGirl.cs:251`) — the only one with a positive seduction/lover gimmick, and the only one that ENRAGEs (full-heal + permanent damage boost) when you kill its child (`SlimeGirl.cs:619-629`).
- **Censored** inverts the counter direction: its sacrifice command makes the counter go *up* at the cost of killing your own worker (`Censored.cs:260,273-277`), and it is the only ALEPH that becomes invincible mid-animation (`Censored.cs:150`).

## Cross-tier gradient

### What escalates as you climb

**Escape likelihood — the clearest monotonic axis.** ZAYIN is overwhelmingly non-escaping: 10 of 11 carry `escapeable=false, qliphoth=0` with no Escape override (OneBadManyGood, DontTouchMe, LightsHammer, Wellcheers, Bald, Fairy, ResetMirror, IronMaiden, YouMustHappy, PromiseAndFaith). TETH introduces a real but small qliphoth counter (1–4) on 12 of 22, of which roughly half actually call `Escape()` at zero (StraitJacket, Bunny, Matchgirl, Ppodae) while the rest fire an in-cell skill (1.76mhz `MakeNoise`, Sakura `ActivateSkill`, LadyLookingAtWall discharge). HE keeps the counter small (max 1–2) and splits 12 of 20 between Escape-on-zero (Helper, Butterfly, Scarecrow, Rudolph, LookAtMe, Porccu) and stationary lethal states (NamelessFetus `StartCrying`, GalaxyBoy `_crying`, Freischutz `ShootBullet`, SingingMachine). WAW makes counter-zero breach the dominant pattern — 20 of 26 gate on `ActivateQliphothCounter` (excluding a duplicate and the two by-proxy kit-tools; see the WAW profile correction) — and ALEPH makes it universal: 6 of 6 override `ActivateQliphothCounter()` to call `Escape()` directly (NullThing, SilentOrchestra, DangoCreature, Censored, BlueStar, SlimeGirl). So "will it leave the cell" climbs cleanly from "almost never" to "always."

**Damage type and scale.** ZAYIN harm is mostly single-target and often reward-shaped (8 of 11 pair a benefit with a downside: Wellcheers heal-vs-`Die()`, Fairy 10%/s heal that kills on idle, PromiseAndFaith weapon gamble). TETH adds scripted single-target kills (11 of 22: SpiderMom `MakeCocoon`, BeautyBeast `hp=0`, Baku, Bunny's 500 R one-shot). HE keeps instant-death (11 of 20) but begins widening reach — ScytheClock kills a *random facility-wide* agent, SnowQueen kills two. WAW pushes lethality to bypass HP entirely on 11 of 26 (Shark's fixed R 150 charge, FengYun's R 250, ReverseClock's 50% facility-wide `Die()`-or-`Panic()`) and adds child-spawn/mind-control delivery (QueenBeeWorker self-replication, BlackSwanSister, AuthorNote drafting 5 staff to kill each other). ALEPH tops it: every member reaches the whole facility on breach, with high cascade on 6 of 6 — corridor chain-kills (NullThing LV2), 100% energy drain + execute-all-panickers (SilentOrchestra finale), self-propagating infestation (SlimeGirl pawns become slimes), and spawned CensoredChild creatures.

**Mechanical / state complexity.** ZAYIN state is per-interaction counters and timers, never a breach phase (DontTouchMe's 10s click queue, IronMaiden's `_tickCount`, YouMustHappy's 6-use limit). TETH and HE add the qliphoth hook plus a single uncontrollable/roaming state. WAW layers cross-facility teleport state machines (SnowWhite `CastTeleport`, Alriune `GetTeleportTargets`, MagicalGirl_2 portal charges) and bidirectional cascade coupling (8 of 26). ALEPH is uniformly a multi-form escalating state machine — 6 of 6 have per-phase stats: NullThing's 5-form `NothingForm` enum, SilentOrchestra's 5-movement concert, DangoCreature's 4-level eat-to-grow, SlimeGirl's 6-phase + 2-state. This axis escalates cleanly.

**Containment & cascade difficulty.** ZAYIN is self-resolving by construction (no breach to suppress). TETH adds self-recontaining breaches (5 of 22: Matchgirl one-blast self-suppress, Ppodae eat-and-return, Dummy harmless walk). HE introduces genuine roaming suppression targets (8 of 20 hunt across rooms: Helper, Rudolph, Butterfly, Lumberjack, Scarecrow). WAW makes roaming the norm (14 of 26) plus cascade coupling that drives neighbors toward breach (BigBadWolf draining others' counters, RedHood coupled to BigBadWolf, Yin/Yang mutual trigger). ALEPH is hard recontainment on 5 of 6 with facility-wide cascade on all 6.

**Hidden-knowledge burden.** This rises but less cleanly. TETH already demands deep stat-gate knowledge (7 of 22: SpiderMom's lethal Prudence work shows *good* probability, ArmorCreature's B-work prob is 0 at all levels, Baku/BloodBath lethal below stat level 2). HE adds inverted work puzzles (6 of 20: Scarecrow needs *low*-Prudence workers, Butterfly punishes *high* Fortitude, LookAtMe punishes the instinctive "watch it" action). The burden is arguably as high at TETH/HE as anywhere — DontTouchMe (ZAYIN) carries the single highest knowledge burden in its tier despite never breaching.

### Where adjacent tiers blur

- **ZAYIN ↔ ALEPH (the sharpest discontinuity): PinkCorps.** The lone ZAYIN that truly breaches — dual escapeable flag, real qliphoth=3, and at zero `ActivateQliphothCounter()` → `MakeBlackCorps` spawns **4 ALEPH-class BlackCorps** (`BlackCorps.cs:46 RiskLevel.ALEPH`) that explode Sefira-wide (W 30–40) and cascade-breach neighbors. Mechanically PinkCorps belongs to the WAW/ALEPH cascade family, not its labeled tier.
- **ZAYIN ↔ TETH: LightsHammer and DontTouchMe.** LightsHammer is a counter-breach *tool* keyed off other creatures' escapes (`GetConditionScore>=10`), more like TETH's tool sub-group than a hazard. DontTouchMe's facility-wide `KillAllWorker` + `SetAllQliphothCounter` reach exceeds most of TETH despite zero escape capability.
- **TETH ↔ HE: SmallBird.** Explicitly flagged as "far beyond TETH's usual scope" — breaches off *any* facility panic, flips to 800–1200 Mad mode, and feeds the Apocalypse Bird ordeal. It reads as a HE/WAW cascade creature wearing a TETH label. Sakura's facility-wide attract-and-sacrifice with mind-control also overlaps HE's mobile-threat profile.
- **TETH ↔ WAW (tool sub-group blur):** DesireHeart, Theresia, HealthBracelet, JusticeReceiver, ProphecyOfSkin (TETH kits) sit in the same non-containment, equip-or-channel category as WAW's MeatIdol, HellTrain, Yang, ReverseClock. Tool risk is decoupled from tier — these are graded by buff strength and timing-window lethality, not breach difficulty.
- **HE ↔ TETH/WAW: Shelter and Freischutz.** Shelter is "HE-tier in name only" — a defensive tool that heals and *reduces* facility risk by zeroing another creature's counter, overlapping ZAYIN's benign units and WAW's Yang. Freischutz has the second-highest HE counter but never escapes (fires a single sniper shot), reading more like a TETH in-cell discharge.
- **WAW ↔ TETH: Yang and OrangeTree.** Yang's stat `riskLevel` is literally **2 (TETH)** despite its WAW display label — it deals no offensive damage, only heals and reflects. OrangeTree is inert: it reuses the `DummyCreature` tutorial script verbatim, identical to TETH's Dummy — two "harmless walk to entry node" creatures three tiers apart.

### Is it monotonic? No — clean on some axes, broken on others

The gradient is **clean and monotonic on escape likelihood and on multi-phase state complexity**: non-escaping → small-counter → universal-Escape, and single-state → roaming → full multi-form state machine, with prevalence backing each step (10/11, 12/22, 12/20, 20/26, 6/6 for the escape axis).

It is **not monotonic on lethality, reach, or knowledge burden**, and there are clear discontinuities in both directions:

- **A ZAYIN with ALEPH-level cascade: PinkCorps** — spawns ALEPH creatures, the single biggest tier-vs-behavior mismatch in the set.
- **A ZAYIN with facility-wide reach and the highest knowledge burden: DontTouchMe** — `KillAllWorker` and run-ending `ExitGame` from a non-escaping box.
- **A TETH with WAW/HE cascade: SmallBird** — passive-trigger breach, Mad mode, Apocalypse Bird feeder.
- **An "ALEPH" whose danger is purely numeric/timing, not combat: none fully** — but **DangoCreature** breaks the ALEPH shape (only 1000 HP vs 1500–2200, can be *de-leveled* by damage, no force-panic on entry), and **SilentOrchestra** breaks ALEPH's hard-recontainment norm by auto-returning via `ForcelyReturn` with no player suppression.
- **Benign creatures scattered across every tier:** ResetMirror/Bald (ZAYIN), Dummy/ShyThing (TETH), Shelter (HE), Yang/OrangeTree (WAW) — the "harmless or net-positive" property does not respect tier at all.

So the honest summary: tier predicts *escape likelihood and structural complexity* well, predicts *roaming/cascade reach* moderately (with the WAW→ALEPH step being the firmest), and predicts *raw lethality and knowledge burden* poorly — TETH and HE already field instant-kill and inverted-puzzle traps as punishing as anything above them, and each tier carries at least one outlier (PinkCorps, SmallBird, DangoCreature, the tool sub-groups) that sits a full tier or more away from its label.

## Per-tier confidence — how strongly the behavioral pattern holds

This verdict measures **pattern strength**: how tightly a tier's in-scope creatures converge on a shared behavioral design. It is judged on prevalence concentration, and is kept separate from synthesis quality-control (the two corrected counts, noted at the end).

- **ZAYIN — crisp.** The tier converges hard on a single idea: *danger comes from interacting with the box, not from it breaching.* 10 of 11 are non-escaping and 11 of 11 deliver harm only through the work/interaction window. The lone structural exception (PinkCorps) is dramatic but isolated, and the secondary shapes (reward-traps 8/11, interaction-counters 8/11) reinforce rather than dilute the core.
- **TETH — crisp.** 20 of 22 are work-conditional threats — the hazard is gated on *how* you work them — and 14 of 22 still cannot breach at all. The organizing principle is strong; the specific punishment (panic, single-target death, counter-gimmick) varies, which is the intended spread, not incoherence.
- **HE — loose (transitional).** This is the one tier where risk looks assigned across a band rather than by a dominant rule: no hallmark exceeds ~60% (qliphoth-driven 12/20, scripted instant-death 11/20, non-breaching 11/20, mobile-on-breach 8/20). HE reads as the blend tier — roughly half still behave like in-cell TETH boxes, half like mobile WAW breachers. A well-grounded "loosely defined," not a failure to find a pattern.
- **WAW — crisp spine, deliberately diverse body.** The organizing principle is strong — 20 of 26 gate their breach/danger on a Qliphoth counter, and the tier is explicitly "a catalogue of management puzzles" — but the implementations fan out (mood-lever 14/26, mobile breach 14/26, scripted death 11/26, child-delegated combat 7/26, cascade coupling 8/26), so no single *mechanic* dominates below the spine. Coherent at the meta level, heterogeneous in detail.
- **ALEPH — crisp (small n).** The crispest tier by concentration: all 6 share the full template — qliphoth-zero breach, work-outcome counter drain, multi-form state machine, facility-wide reach, high cascade — at 6/6. n=6 limits statistical weight, and two members break secondary norms (SilentOrchestra auto-recontains; DangoCreature is under-statted and de-levelable), but the core design language is unanimous.
